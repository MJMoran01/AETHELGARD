## 1. Does the dataset actually support the physics premise?

**Verdict: UNDECIDABLE on the raw-counts question — with strong adverse
evidence — and REFUTED on the "labels support the pipeline" question.**

Attacks run:

- **The data is not in the repo at all.** `HUMS-X-ray-Dataset/HighLow/`
  contains exactly one file: `convertToJPG.m`. All TIFs are gitignored
  (`.gitignore` lines under "Data files"), and the `NotThreats/` and
  `ThreatsRGB/` directories listed in `README.md:160-164` do not exist in
  this checkout. Every claim about "82 paired 16-bit grayscale" TIFs
  (`README.md:171`) is currently unverifiable from the repository. The
  dataset's own `Aethelgard_Master_Design.md` is **0 bytes**.
- **Fingerprints of vendor post-processing, not raw photon counts.** The
  committed debug artifact `outputs/debug/01_raw_input.png` (generated by
  `scripts/demo_debug.py` on a machine that had the data) shows the
  low-energy image spanning exactly `[0, 65535]` — the full 16-bit range
  hitting both rails exactly, the classic signature of contrast-stretched/
  normalized export rather than linear detector counts — plus a visible
  dead-pixel column. The high-energy image spans `[1186, 51415]`.
  `dataset.py:13-22` asserts flatly "Both are 16-bit grayscale TIF files
  representing raw photon counts" — nothing in the repo verifies this, and
  the Beer-Lambert linearization (`preprocessing.py:325-327`), the
  Poisson-noise rationale for the pre-log blur (`preprocessing.py:25-44`),
  and the entire Alvarez-Macovski premise are all conditional on it. If the
  vendor already applied log/gamma/window mapping, `L = -ln(I/I0)` is not
  `μt` and every downstream "physics" quantity is a nonlinear function of
  arbitrary display units.
- **Provenance:** the dataset is from Hacettepe University / MS Spektral
  (single-view dual-channel scanner exports) — scanner exports of this kind
  are typically flat-fielded and normalized, not raw counts.
- **The annotations do not label the dual-energy data.** All 150 XMLs in
  `HUMS-X-ray-Dataset/Annotation/` declare `<depth>3</depth>` and reference
  **PNG** filenames (`pngImg_NN.png`, and `image_0_NNNN.png` under a folder
  literally named `augmented_knifeset`, e.g.
  `Annotation/image_0_1000.xml:2-4`). These are labels for the pseudocolor
  RGB / augmented set — a set not present in the repo — not for
  `*_hi.tif`/`*_lo.tif` pairs. No mapping from annotation to TIF pair exists
  anywhere in the repo. The physics pipeline therefore currently has **zero
  usable labels**.
- The bundled `convertToJPG.m` (`HighLow/convertToJPG.m`) reads the TIFs
  into a variable named `rgbImg` with a commented-out `rgb2gray` call — weak
  but adverse evidence that the upstream authors handled these files as
  3-channel.

**Important caveat in the prompt's favor-of-the-author direction:** the
model code does *not* train on pseudocolored RGB — `dataset.py:66-96` loads
`*_lo.tif`/`*_hi.tif` pairs, and `01_raw_input.png` shows two genuinely
distinct grayscale energy channels (mean L_low/L_high ≈ 1.67 per
`07_sanity_check.png`). The worst version of the "trains on RGB" hypothesis
is not what the code does. The failure is subtler: two energy channels
exist, but whether they are linear transmission data is unestablished, and
the labels belong to a different image set.

**Cheapest settling instrument:** obtain the TIFs; in a flat air region,
test the Poisson signature (variance ≈ mean across repeated flat patches /
check integer count statistics), and check whether `pngImg_NN` indices map
1:1 to TIF pair indices. One afternoon.

## 2. Does the pipeline achieve material discrimination on real data?

**Verdict: REFUTED — by the repo's own committed evidence.**

- `outputs/debug/05_zeff_histogram.png`: on a real bag image **visibly
  containing metal blades** (see `01_raw_input.png`), the entire Z_eff
  distribution is a single narrow peak at ~9-13, mean 10.4, **max 14.4**.
  Iron is 26. Not one pixel reaches the "Metals (Z>18)" band the plot
  itself draws. The pipeline's flagship output — "metals (bright) vs
  organics (dark)" (`visualize_physics.py:11`) — does not happen on real
  data.
- `outputs/debug/07_sanity_check.png` — the project's own sanity check —
  prints the warning `Z_eff range unusual: [0.1, 14.4]`.
- `outputs/debug/04_aspace_scatter.png`: the A-space cloud is essentially
  **one ray** (theta ~ 0.85). This is mathematically forced by the
  uncalibrated init: with `A1 = 1.5*L_L - 0.5*L_H`, `A2 = 0.5*L_L + 1.0*L_H`
  (`physics_head.py:537-558`) and L_L ~ 1.7*L_H on this data, A1/A2 is
  nearly constant regardless of material. The "Salt & Sugar" separation
  exists only in the synthetic unit test (`physics_head.py:870-910`), where
  the answer is baked into the inputs.
- Same artifact: **k-NN outliers: 48,342** — the ASpaceNoiseFilter flags
  tens of thousands of pixels of one image as "isolated noise" and would
  replace them with neighbors' values. Its own premise ("noise produces
  ISOLATED points", `physics_head.py:101-104`) is refuted at that rate.

Strongest surviving counter-argument: the coefficients were never
calibrated, and calibration might spread the A-space fan. That is precisely
the point — the discrimination claim was recorded (README, docstrings, demo
labels) **before** any evidence of it existed, and the existing evidence is
negative.

## 4. Physical validity of the individual claims

### 4a. Z_eff exponent and the wedge bounds — **REFUTED (internal numerical contradiction)**

The functional form `Z_eff = c*(A1/A2)^(1/3)` is a legitimate approximation
(a constant prefactor is the right shape for an unknown basis-function
ratio). But the recorded justifications contradict the code's own numbers:

- `physics_head.py:690-691`: "Default scaling aims to put carbon ~6, iron
  ~26." With `z_scale=10`, carbon at 6 requires A1/A2 = 0.216 and iron at 26
  requires A1/A2 = 17.6 — no derivation exists that the decomposition
  produces those ratios, and the real-data histogram (item 2) shows it does
  not.
- `physics_head.py:125-131` claims `theta_min=0.05 ~ Z=1` and
  `theta_max=1.50 ~ Z=92`. Under the code's own Z_eff formula: Z=1 => A1/A2
  = (1/10)^3 = 10^-3 => theta = **0.001 rad**, not 0.05; Z=92 => A1/A2 =
  9.2^3 ~ 779 => theta = **1.5695 rad**, not 1.50. Conversely theta=1.50
  corresponds to Z_eff ~ **24** — so the "uranium bound" would clamp *iron
  and above*, silently capping exactly the high-Z threat signal the project
  exists to find. The stated physical justification and the constants
  disagree in both directions; under the Glass Box policy that makes both
  numbers unexplained constants.

### 4b. Ratio-gradient "thickness invariance" — **REFUTED as a general claim**

The derivation (`Aethelgard_Design.md:101-132`, `physics_head.py:704-719`)
holds only for a **single homogeneous material** in the monoenergetic
limit. Counterexample from the project's own motivating scenario (Section
1.2, superposition): two overlapping layers give R = (mu1_L*t1 +
mu2_L*t2)/(mu1_H*t1 + mu2_H*t2), which depends on t1/t2 — so grad(R) != 0
wherever the thickness of *one* layer varies behind another, i.e.
everywhere in a cluttered bag. The feature detects material edges only in
the uncluttered case the architecture claims to transcend. Additionally,
`R = L_L/(L_H + 1e-6)` (`physics_head.py:729`) has no low-signal mask: in
air, both L's are noise near zero and R (hence grad(R)) is noise-dominated
— AGENTS.md constraint 5 explicitly demands an "explicit mask or clamp" for
exactly this "division by zero noise" case; an epsilon in the denominator
bounds the value but not the noise amplification. Nothing masks
low-attenuation pixels.

### 4c. Pre-log Gaussian blur as Poisson handling — **CONFIRMED as a permitted named stand-in, with an unstated defect**

AGENTS.md (`AGENTS.md:296-300`) explicitly blesses "Gaussian blur as a
Poisson-noise stand-in" when named, and `preprocessing.py:25-58` names it.
Attack that partially lands: blurring intensities across an edge before the
log biases attenuation downward there (Jensen: -ln of a mean != mean of
-ln) — this is the "exponential edge-gradient effect," which is literally
reference 12 of the design doc (`Aethelgard_Design.md:493`) and is never
connected to this design choice. It also directly softens the very edges
grad(R) is supposed to detect (the module's own test 3 measures the
smoothing, `preprocessing.py:479-484`). And the whole Poisson rationale is
conditional on item 1's unresolved raw-counts question. Surviving
counter-argument: for a first pass with sigma=1 the bias is small and the
trade-off is at least documented.

### 4d. I0 = per-image max — **REFUTED as a basis for cross-image physics**

Two different implementations both self-normalize by per-image brightest
pixel, but at different points in the pipeline: the PyTorch preprocessor
(`preprocessing.py:239-243` estimate, applied at `preprocessing.py:301-310`)
estimates I0 from the **blurred** signal, while `DualEnergyDataset`
(`dataset.py:174-184`) uses the **unblurred** loaded arrays directly. Both
are per-image-relative regardless of the blur-timing difference: L, A1,
A2, and Z_eff all end up **image-relative** quantities. A "Z_eff = 10.4"
from one image and another
are not on the same scale (fails if any image lacks clean air, saturates,
or has hot pixels under `per_image_max`). The design doc's own
self-calibration section (Section 6.2) proposes belt/air anchoring instead;
the implemented shortcut was never surfaced as a decision. Also directly
falsifies `preprocessing.py:111-112`: it *is* effectively a per-image
learnable-free calibration whose assumption ("brightest pixel = unattenuated
beam") is asserted, not checked.

## 5. Black-box components despite the Glass Box policy

**Verdict: REFUTED (policy already violated by today's code) — three
concrete instances.**

1. **The heuristic polynomial coefficients are the banned "stand-in doing
   real work."** The design doc itself calls them "Placeholder values -
   typically derived from Phantom data" (`Aethelgard_Design.md:246`). The
   teaching mandate bans exactly this: "Randomly-initialized stand-ins for
   a component that should do real work are not [acceptable]"
   (`AGENTS.md:299-300`). Worse, `demo_debug.py:594` labels this
   uncalibrated pass "**BHC decomposition**" and `demo_debug.py:298-304`
   renders it as "Z_eff WITH BHC (Correct - Use this!)" against a "naive"
   strawman — the committed artifact `03_bhc_comparison.png` presents an
   uncalibrated guess as a validated beam-hardening correction. There is no
   beam-hardening *correction* anywhere: no phantom, no spectrum, no
   water-equivalent fit.
2. **ASpaceNoiseFilter** (`physics_head.py:73-423`): unregistered in the
   design record (item 3), **non-differentiable** (numpy/scipy with
   `.detach().cpu()` at `physics_head.py:377-378` — inside the
   "differentiable physics engine" premise), **nondeterministic**
   (unseeded `np.random.choice` at `physics_head.py:284-286`), and carrying
   a real bug: when subsampling, *all* points are queried against a
   100k-point subsampled tree, but the code still assumes "first neighbor
   is the point itself" (`physics_head.py:291-297`) — false for every point
   not in the subsample, so in-tree points get a spurious 0-distance
   self-match column while out-of-tree points don't, contaminating the MAD
   threshold. The 48,342-outlier figure in `04_aspace_scatter.png` is the
   visible consequence. Constants `mad_multiplier=3.0`,
   `subsample_threshold=100000`, and the theta bounds (see 4a) are
   unexplained.
   `__main__` (Tests 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8) prints a warning symbol on failure
   but **never asserts** — the script exits 0 regardless of whether salt
   beats sugar or the rivet survives. Only `preprocessing.py:487-489` and
   the shape check in Test 1 (`physics_head.py:864`) actually assert —
   every *semantic physics* check in the rest of the `__main__` block is
   print-only and cannot fail the script.

## 6. Research-plan coherence: is there a path to a defensible result?

**Verdict: REFUTED as currently scoped.**

- **The entire learning half of the architecture does not exist.** No
  VisionHead, no PhysicsAttention, no AethelgardNet, no training loop, no
  loss, no split, no metric — anywhere. `aethelgard/__init__.py` exports
  four symbols across three conceptual categories: `RawToLogAttenuation`
  (preprocessing), `PhysicsHead`, and `DualEnergyDataset` +
  `SyntheticDualEnergyDataset` (dataset). The README's
  "[Future: ResNet backbone]" (`README.md:53`) has no tracker issue, no
  plan document (the active tracker holds only the bootstrap issue), and no
  milestone.
- **The stated next step is under-determined.** `TO-DO.txt` (in full):
  "Optimize polynomial coefficients via backprop instead of using
  initialized guesses." Backprop against *what*? The only labels are knife
  boxes for a different image set (item 1). A detection loss cannot
  identify the 12 decomposition coefficients — any invertible 2->2 mixing
  of (A1,A2) composed with a compensating downstream network yields the
  same loss, so the learned "A1/A2" lose their claimed physical meaning and
  the glass box quietly becomes a learned 2-channel nonlinearity wearing
  physics-named variables. Identifiability would require calibration
  targets (phantoms, known-material anchors per design Section 6.2) that
  are nowhere scoped.
- **Scale:** 82 image pairs (claimed; unverifiable, files absent), 150
  annotations for a different modality, license/provenance flagged as an
  open risk by the repo's own AGENTS.md constraint 4. No plan addresses any
  of it.
- `Simplifying assumptions.txt` honestly lists two gaps (fan-beam geometry,
  detector sensitivity) — but not the larger ones found here: raw-counts
  assumption, label mismatch, per-image I0, ratio-gradient superposition
  failure, uncalibrated coefficients presented as BHC.

today:

1. **"The TIFs are linear raw photon counts"** — asserted at
   `dataset.py:13`, verified nowhere, conditions everything.

   image-relative (4d).

   `INORGANIC_MAX=18` at `demo_debug.py:67-71`, wedge bounds) inherits it.

   ~13% of pixels with neighbor values.

   uncalibrated guess as the beam-hardening correction the design doc's
